Bold is for the number sets and for nothing else: \mathbf{Z}, \mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{R}, \mathbf{C}, never \mathbb. A space, a set, a subspace, a cone or a map named by a plain capital stays a plain capital. A letter drawn in the script face is \mathscr, so the space of continuous functions is \mathscr{C} and the algebra of endomorphisms is \mathscr{L}.

Copy the letter that is printed rather than the one you expect. The curly rho is \varrho and the straight one is \rho. The script l is \ell. A ring drawn over a letter is \mathring and not a dot, and a small circle written after one and high is a superscript, so the interior of C is \mathring{C} and its polar is C^\circ. The inequality signs are the slanted \leqslant and \geqslant. A bar drawn through a relation is part of it, so read \not= and \not\subset where the bar is there and = and \subset where it is not. Angle brackets around a pair are \langle and \rangle. A subscript set in parentheses keeps them, as in u_{(\mathbf{C})}.

Keep § and no. as they are printed. An accent belongs to the letter it is drawn over, and a mark drawn wide enough to span an expression is \widehat, \widetilde or \overline rather than the narrow form.
