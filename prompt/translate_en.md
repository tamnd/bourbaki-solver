English rules.

The passage in front of you is French, and this is the one direction where a
published English Bourbaki exists to be matched. Algebra, Theory of Sets and Lie
Groups and Lie Algebras were put into English by Springer, and the volumes that
come this way are the ones that never were. Write the English those translations
write, so that a reader moving between them does not feel the seam.

Write the mathematical English of a textbook. Not a word for word rendering of
the French, and not the English of a newspaper.

The statement words are fixed: Théorème is Theorem, Proposition is Proposition,
Lemme is Lemma, Corollaire is Corollary, Définition is Definition, Remarque is
Remark, Exemple is Example, Scholie is Scholium, Exercice is Exercise. A heading
reading `Théorème 3` becomes `Theorem 3`, with the number where it is.

The structural words are fixed too, because the cross references are built out
of them: `Chapitre` is Chapter, `no` is No., `p.` is p., and `§` stays `§`.
`cf.` stays `cf.` and `resp.` stays `resp.`, since Bourbaki writes both in
English as well.

Proper names are left alone. Cartan is Cartan, Weil is Weil, Dieudonné is
Dieudonné, Grothendieck is Grothendieck. Keep the accents on a name.

French sets off a defined term in italics where English does the same, so keep
the marks where they are rather than moving them onto a different word.

The French of these volumes is careful about two things English translators of
Bourbaki are careful about in turn. `application` is a mapping, not an
application, and Bourbaki's English uses mapping throughout. `anneau` is a ring,
and in these volumes a ring is associative with a unit unless the text says
otherwise, so do not soften it to a structure. Where the glossary below gives a
term, the glossary wins over both of these.

Do not modernise. Bourbaki's English is the English of the printing, so write
mapping rather than function where the French writes application, and write
neighbourhood rather than neighborhood: the Springer volumes are set in British
spelling and these volumes sit beside them.
