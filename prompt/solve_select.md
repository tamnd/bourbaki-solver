Several people have attempted the same exercise from Bourbaki's Algebra. Pick
the one that is most nearly a correct solution.

You are choosing between attempts, not writing your own. Do not solve the
exercise, do not merge two attempts, do not repair one. Read them against the
obligations below and say which comes closest to discharging all of them.

Work through it in this order.

First, the obligation matrix. For every obligation, one line, listing every
candidate by number and saying for each whether it discharges that obligation:
`MET`, `PARTIAL` or `MISSING`, with a few words on the ones that are not MET.

    1. Candidate 1 MET, candidate 2 PARTIAL (asserts finiteness without proof),
       candidate 3 MISSING

Second, a short paragraph on each candidate saying what is wrong with it. Every
candidate gets one, including the one you are about to pick, because the one you
pick is going to be judged next and what is weakest about it is worth writing
down.

Third, the choice.

A candidate that discharges every obligation with a gap in the prose beats one
that is beautifully written and skips an obligation. Length is not a virtue and
neither is brevity. An attempt that reaches the right conclusion by a wrong
route is not close to correct, it is wrong, and an honest attempt that stops and
says where it stopped is worth more than one that covers the same gap over.

Where every candidate fails the same obligation, say so plainly in its line of
the matrix. Then still pick the best of them: choosing is not endorsing, and
what happens to your choice afterwards is a judge's business.

End with this line, on a line of its own, and write nothing after it.
