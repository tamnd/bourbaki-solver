This volume prints its page number at the foot of the page and its running head does not carry it. Everything above holds. The rules below are about that number, and they are here because the shared rules ask for the running head and say nothing about the foot, so a reading that follows them alone comes back with the page number nowhere on it.

That costs more here than it would elsewhere. This volume has no text layer behind it, so the number the page prints is the only record of it there will ever be, and the page map is fitted from that number. Without it the fit has nothing to work from and the volume cannot be assembled however well the body of every page was read.

- Transcribe the page number printed at the foot as the last line of the page, on a line of its own, as the digits alone. Do not write the word page in front of it, do not put it in brackets, do not set it as a heading and do not pad it.
- It goes last, under the footnotes, so that it is the final line of the page whatever else the foot carries.
- Where the page prints no number at the foot, write nothing. A page that opens a chapter often prints none, and a blank leaf never does. Do not work out what the number would have been and do not carry one over from the page before.
- The number at the foot is the page number even when a number of the same value is printed in the running head or stands in the body. Read it off the foot of the image and not off anything else on the page.
